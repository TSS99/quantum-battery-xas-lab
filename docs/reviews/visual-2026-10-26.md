# Visual review of the beamline twin, 2026-10-26

Written by GPT (gpt-5.6-sol through the Codex CLI, read only) from six screenshots of v1.7.0. The v1.8.0 changes answer the parts of it marked H1 to H10 in the plan.

## (a) Ten biggest visual problems and exact fixes

1. **The UI overwhelms the instrument.** On desktop, the beamline receives barely half the width; the legend consumes another large portion of the viewport.

   **Fix:** Make the 3D view the primary canvas—minimum 65–70% width. Collapse the left explanation into a 360 px drawer and the right controls into a 320 px inspector. Put “Acquire” and mode selection in one persistent toolbar.

2. **Lighting is flat, overexposed and game-like.** White surfaces clip while cyan bloom removes edge definition.

   **Fix:** Use ACES filmic tone mapping at exposure 0.85–0.95. Start with:

   - 4300 K ceiling area lights: 450 lux
   - 5000 K camera-side fill: 100–130 lux
   - 6000 K rear rim: 150–180 lux
   - Neutral HDRI/environment intensity: 0.25–0.35
   - Contact shadows/AO: 0.4–0.6 m radius, restrained intensity
   - Bloom only on selected emissive objects: threshold 1.3, strength 0.10–0.15, radius 0.25

   Keep walls below approximately 80% display white; reserve true white for lamps and highlights.

3. **Most hardware reads as the same grey plastic.**

   **Fix:** Establish a disciplined PBR material library:

   - Brushed stainless steel: metalness 1.0, roughness 0.25–0.35
   - Bead-blasted aluminium: 0.9 / 0.38
   - Black anodised aluminium: 0.75 / 0.32
   - Powder-coated cabinets: 0.0 / 0.48
   - Copper cooling parts: 1.0 / 0.22
   - Silicon crystals: 0.0 / 0.16, dark neutral grey
   - Viewing glass: transmission 1.0, IOR 1.5, roughness 0.04

   Add subtle brushed normals, machining direction, fasteners, flange seams and equipment decals. Avoid broad edge wear; this should look maintained, not weathered.

4. **The geometry resembles display cases rather than beamline vacuum equipment.**

   **Fix:** Replace transparent box housings with credible vacuum chambers: CF flanges, bellows, gate valves, pumps, feedthroughs, cooling manifolds and removable access covers. Keep cutaway shells as an optional teaching layer, not the default physical object.

5. **Scale is inconsistent and difficult to read.** Chambers, cabinets, detector heads and the room have no common dimensional logic.

   **Fix:** Lock the beam axis to a stated datum, for example 1.35 m above floor. Use plausible dimensions consistently: 0.25–0.50 m ion-chamber active lengths, approximately 1–1.5 m monochromator vessel, 25–40 mm beam pipes, and standard 19-inch electronics racks. Add a human silhouette or 0.5 m scale bar in the whole-beamline view.

6. **The cameras make the line look miniature.** The whole view is very wide and distant; close-ups show strong perspective distortion.

   **Fix:** Use a 38–45° vertical FOV for navigation and 25–32° for component close-ups. Keep close-up cameras around a 50–70 mm full-frame equivalent and move the camera rather than zooming through FOV. Frame the selected component at a rule-of-thirds point with the upstream and downstream beam path still visible.

7. **Floating labels obscure the hardware and compete equally for attention.**

   **Fix:** Show at most four labels: selected component, immediate upstream/downstream components and one contextual object. Fade all others to markers. Use collision avoidance, short orthogonal leaders and edge docking. Never place labels over the beam, sample or detector face.

8. **Photon effects are visually noisy and weakly informative.** Green flashes, random dots, cyan edges and bloom all compete with the actual beam path.

   **Fix:** Give each packet a stable meaning. At 9.000 keV, incident packets should have one consistent false colour; Cu Kα, Kβ and scattered packets should use their calculated energies plus different shapes. Reduce packet size by roughly 40%, eliminate ambient sparkles and terminate packets at absorption or detector intersection. Put artistic multipliers such as “×2” directly beside the affected population.

9. **The room lacks visual grounding.** Repeating wall fasteners, bright floor glare and identical cabinets make the scene feel procedural.

   **Fix:** Darken the floor to graphite grey, add restrained reflections, cable trays, cooling hoses, rack ventilation and contact shadows. Reduce wall-panel contrast by about 50%. Give major systems distinct silhouettes instead of repeating the same pedestal-and-box construction.

10. **The phone layout is a desktop page stacked vertically.** The introduction consumes the first screen, tabs overflow, and oversized labels cover the model.

   **Fix:** On widths below 600 px, collapse the introduction to a two-line summary, use a 60–70 vh viewport, and move controls into a draggable bottom sheet. Use scroll-snapped mode tabs, 44 px minimum touch targets, labels capped at 14 px, and a maximum of two simultaneous callouts. Provide one-finger orbit, two-finger pan and a visible “Reset view.”

## (b) Physically wrong or misleading

1. **The open beam path is the largest credibility problem.** Synchrotron mirrors and monochromators normally operate in vacuum, connected by beam pipes, bellows and valves. The current long open-air gaps and glass display cases suggest laboratory optics.

2. **The ion chambers do not look like ion chambers.** \(I_0\), \(I_t\) and \(I_\mathrm{ref}\) should read as sealed, inline gas cells with entrance/exit windows, electrodes, gas connections and meaningful active length—not thin transverse posts.

3. **The visible glowing beam can overpower the disclaimer.** X-rays do not illuminate air or leave a luminous green trail. “Realistic” mode should hide the beam completely except at a scintillator, detector response or diagnostic screen.

4. **The monochromator needs a clear fixed-exit ray path.** Show the first Bragg reflection, second reflection and vertical offset explicitly, including coupled crystal motion during an energy scan. Otherwise the two crystals resemble generic mirrors. The displayed Si(111) angle of 12.690° at 9.000 keV is correct.

5. **“The crystals pass one photon in \(2\times10^4\)” is too universal.** Monochromator throughput depends on source bandwidth, divergence, reflectivity, acceptance and detuning. Label this as a visualization sampling ratio, not an intrinsic crystal property.

6. **The mirrors do not visibly operate at grazing incidence.** Show the milliradian-scale deflection, beam footprint and sagittal/tangential focusing. A straight central glow through a box suggests normal-incidence optics.

7. **Fluorescence and scatter appear interchangeable.** For a Cu foil just above its 8.979 keV K edge, dominant fluorescence should be around 8.05 keV Kα and 8.91 keV Kβ; elastic scatter remains at approximately 9.00 keV. They need distinct energies, origins and detector outcomes.

8. **The fluorescence geometry is conceptually right but visually ambiguous.** A sample near 45° and detector near 90° to the incident beam is credible. Make the sample normal, beam axis, detector axis and solid-angle cone visible so this is understood rather than merely stated.

9. **Packet trajectories sometimes appear to pass through opaque hardware or continue after absorption.** Use real ray/object intersections, aperture clipping and termination. Scatter should originate at material, not in empty space.

10. **A live open hutch is misleading.** Show the upstream shutter, personnel-safety interlock state, shielded hutch boundary and beam stop. “Beam on” should be mutually exclusive with an accessible experimental enclosure.

## (c) Five trust builders

1. **Configuration provenance:** expose component model, dimensions, gas, pressure, crystal reflection, mirror coating, sample thickness and revision date. Clearly distinguish “representative teaching model” from “as-built digital twin.”

2. **Traceable physics:** cite the cross-section and optical-constant sources; show equations and assumptions behind transmission, edge step, Bragg angle, bandwidth, harmonic rejection and fluorescence yield.

3. **Engineering overlays:** offer beam height, coordinate axes, apertures, beam envelope, mirror footprints, spot size, divergence and a dimension tool. Include a clean 2D optical schematic beside the 3D view.

4. **Real detector behaviour:** model dark current, Poisson statistics, ion-chamber saturation, amplifier range, fluorescence dead time, pile-up and uncertainty. Display counts, integration time and signal-to-noise—not only ideal curves.

5. **Validation evidence:** include a Cu-foil reference scan with the expected 8.979 keV edge, comparison against measured beamline data, acceptance tolerances and a downloadable configuration/validation report.

## (d) Art direction

1. **Palette:** midnight navy `#111A2B`, graphite `#303941`, stainless `#8C979D`, warm instrument white `#DDE1DF`, interaction cyan `#6FD3EE`, safety amber `#D9A632`. Reserve green for confirmed operational status and red for faults or closed safety states.

2. **Mood:** quiet, precise, controlled and expensive—an industrial metrology suite, not a spacecraft or neon game arena.

3. **References:** contemporary ESRF, Diamond Light Source and NSLS-II beamline photography; ZEISS industrial metrology hardware; Keysight or Rohde & Schwarz instrument interfaces.

4. **Graphic rule:** colour communicates state or measurement only. Structure comes from material, silhouette, lighting and typography—not glowing outlines.

5. **Motion rule:** slow motor travel, mechanically constrained axes and damped camera movement. No idle sparkles, pulsing borders or decorative scanning effects.
